Legacy compatibility

Earlier short surveys retired

CEAM+ has retired its earlier short survey experience. Use the Assessment Center for a more structured exploratory review. Previous survey results, if present on this device, have not been converted or rescored.

CEAM+ assessments provide structured exploratory reviews of the information supplied. They are provisional planning aids, not validated diagnoses, predictions, certifications, or automatic decisions.

Choose an Assessment

Legacy survey results and assessment results use different instruments and methods. Their numbers are not interchangeable and are not compared here.

Survey links now lead to the existing assessments in Assessment Center. The original questionnaires remain available there with the same titles, questions and answer choices. Your historical survey records below are unchanged.

Legacy survey results on this device

This read-only view preserves original records, answers, dates, versions and stored summaries. It does not start, resume, submit, edit, convert or rescore surveys. Nothing is written to browser storage.

Enable JavaScript to read browser-saved legacy survey history. No stored results have been changed.

Open an existing downloaded legacy result locally

The older context engine did not persist results in browser storage. If you kept its JSON download, select that file to inspect or download the original data again. The file remains on this device; it is not uploaded or imported into assessment storage.

What comes next

Use Assessment Center for structured assessments, Marketing Analytics Lab for evidence and data analysis, or the Dashboard for saved assessment history.

Future unscored Project Briefs may describe marketing, research, organization, customer experience, change, data and analytics, or individual decisions. They are planned inputs, not assessments, and will not receive automatic readiness scores. Implementation and follow-up workspaces are also planned.

Research surveys remain legitimate data-collection methods. Retiring this earlier product does not remove that research method or reinterpret historical findings.
