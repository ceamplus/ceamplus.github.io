About Faye Briggs and ClearPathway Systems

My name is Faye Briggs. I am a first-generation college graduate, McNair Scholar, MBA student, future PhD student, researcher, entrepreneur, and creator of CEAM+.

Much of my life has been spent learning how to navigate systems on my own. That shaped the way I see people, work, education, technology, and support.

ClearPathway Systems grew from that experience. CEAM+ became the framework we use to help people and small businesses understand complex situations, make better decisions, identify where AI or another tool fits, and build the support needed for real change.

How CEAM+ Began and How CEAM+ Is Evolving

CEAM+ began with research into how small businesses adopt and respond to emerging technologies, including artificial intelligence, automation, and other forms of digital change. That work revealed a broader pattern I could not ignore: people were not always resisting technology because they opposed it. Often, they were overwhelmed, uncertain, underprepared, or operating without the support, knowledge, resources, or trust needed to adapt effectively.

I began to see that these questions were not only about technology or business. They were connected to a much larger challenge: how people, organizations, communities, economies, and institutions adapt to change without sacrificing human wellbeing, social stability, or the natural systems on which they depend.

That realization expanded CEAM+ beyond a narrow focus on AI adoption. The framework now examines the deeper human and organizational forces that shape change: how people interpret new demands, decide what to trust, respond to uncertainty, preserve agency, resist or accept change, learn from consequences, and determine what should be adopted, sustained, redesigned, simplified, or left behind.

My own evolution has become part of the evolution of CEAM+. I began by asking how organizations could adopt new technologies more effectively. I increasingly found myself asking a different question: What does effective adaptation actually mean if technological or economic progress creates new forms of environmental strain, social instability, dependence, inequality, cognitive overload, or diminished quality of life?

CEAM+ is therefore evolving into a broader interdisciplinary framework for studying adoption, resistance, adaptation, decision-making, technology, human behavior, sustainability, and sustained change. AI remains an important area of application, but it is one part of a larger interconnected system that includes people, organizations, communities, economies, technology, society, and the environment.

Sustainability within CEAM+ is not limited to environmental protection. It also asks whether the systems we create can remain humanly, socially, economically, organizationally, and environmentally sustainable over time. A solution that improves efficiency in the short term but creates greater dependence, waste, inequality, exhaustion, fragility, or environmental harm may not represent meaningful progress.

The larger question guiding CEAM+ is becoming:

How can people, organizations, and societies adapt to change in ways that preserve human agency, strengthen resilience and quality of life, support healthier communities and organizations, and contribute to a more sustainable relationship between technology, society, the economy, and the natural environment?

In this sense, CEAM+ is evolving from a framework concerned primarily with how change is adopted into one that also asks what kinds of change are worth sustaining, for whom, and at what cost.

What we pay attention to

ClearPathway Systems looks at the full picture: the person, the system, the pressure around the decision, and the support needed to make change realistic.

People

We pay attention to how people learn, respond to pressure, ask for help, and rebuild after difficult seasons.

Behavior

We care about what people actually do when they are overwhelmed, unsupported, or trying to change.

Systems

We look at the environment around the person, not just the person standing inside it.

Technology

We want AI and digital tools to make life clearer, not harder to understand.
