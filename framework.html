CEAM+ Layers

CEAM+ began as a four-part model for understanding AI adoption. It has grown into a nine-layer human adaptation framework that examines the realities behind decisions, change, technology, and long-term implementation.

Stable research identity: Understanding how people and organizations adopt, resist, and sustain change in an increasingly AI-driven world.

Core philosophy

CEAM+ does not tell people what to choose. It helps people and organizations understand the conditions shaping their choices so they can exercise greater agency through understanding, reflection, and intentional decision-making.

Adaptive Wisdom Philosophy

CEAM+ is the research and application framework. Adaptive Wisdom is the philosophical and conceptual layer that supports CEAM+ by asking how people and organizations remain wise, resilient, imaginative, and capable of revision when uncertainty cannot be fully controlled.

Adaptive Wisdom adds the uncertainty and resilience lens while CEAM+ organizes adoption, resistance, implementation, learning, and sustained change.

Conditions for Change

Conditions for Change are the conditions that influence a person's or organization's readiness and capacity for change. They help explain why some choices feel possible, difficult, risky, or sustainable.

01

Time

Individuals need time to learn, practice, and recover from mistakes. Organizations need timelines that allow training, review, adoption, and adjustment.

02

Resources

Individuals may need devices, access, money, documentation, or trusted help. Organizations need staffing, budgets, data quality, infrastructure, policies, and implementation support.

03

Cognitive Capacity

Individuals need steps that do not overload attention. Organizations need workflows and communication that reduce confusion instead of adding complexity.

04

Emotional Readiness

Individuals may carry fear, skepticism, fatigue, or hope. Organizations have emotional climates shaped by trust, burnout, leadership credibility, and psychological safety.

05

Social Environment

Individuals are influenced by family, peers, managers, teachers, care teams, and community norms. Organizations are shaped by culture, incentives, leadership behavior, and informal networks.

06

Habits & Routine

Individuals sustain change when it fits real rhythms or replaces a clear pain point. Organizations need change to fit meetings, handoffs, accountability systems, and daily operating routines.

07

Agency

Agency is not the absence of barriers. It is the ability to recognize and act within the meaningful choices that remain despite those barriers.

Conditions influence people. Agency guides responses. Choices become actions, learning, adaptation, and sustained change.

The Original Four CEAM+ Layers

These four layers preserve the original meaning of the CEAM+ name and provide a clear starting point before using the expanded model.

01

Cognitive Layer

How people understand information, manage cognitive load, question assumptions, and make decisions.

  • Do I understand the problem?
  • Am I overwhelmed by too much information?
  • What evidence and information matter most?
02

Ethical Layer

How trust, transparency, privacy, fairness, accountability, and human impact shape responsible choices.

  • Who could benefit or be harmed?
  • How can claims and outputs be verified?
  • Who remains responsible for the decision?
03

Adoption Layer

How people evaluate usefulness, choose an appropriate solution, learn new skills, and put change into practice.

  • What real problem are we solving?
  • What is preventing adoption?
  • What small, measurable step can begin now?
04

Plus Layer

The adaptive support, reflection, training, feedback, and continuous improvement that help positive change last.

  • What support and training are needed?
  • How will progress be measured?
  • What should be adjusted after we learn more?

The Nine Expanded CEAM+ Layers

The expanded framework keeps the original foundation while making emotional, social, practical, and human-AI factors easier to examine.

01

Cognitive Layer

Understand information, mental load, assumptions, evidence, and decisions.

Ask first
  • What information is most important?
  • What assumptions am I making?
  • What evidence supports this decision?
02

Emotional Layer

Notice how fear, uncertainty, shame, frustration, excitement, and confidence affect action.

Ask first
  • What emotions are influencing this decision?
  • Are they helping or limiting progress?
  • What emotional barrier needs support?
03

Agency Layer

Identify choices, influence, responsibility, skills, and available actions.

Ask first
  • What can I control?
  • What can I influence?
  • What action can I take today?
04

Trust & Ethics Layer

Make transparency, privacy, accountability, fairness, risk, and verification visible.

Ask first
  • Why should this be trusted?
  • How can claims be verified?
  • What risks should be considered?
05

Environment Layer

Understand how family, work, culture, systems, resources, and surroundings shape outcomes.

Ask first
  • What supports success?
  • What creates resistance?
  • What practical barriers are being overlooked?
06

AI Solution & Implementation Layer

Select the right tool category and turn intention into a small, measurable, repeatable change.

Ask first
  • What problem needs to be solved?
  • Which tool fits the situation?
  • How will progress be measured?
07

Human-AI Partnership Layer

Define the appropriate roles for AI assistance, human oversight, verification, and judgment.

Ask first
  • What should AI do?
  • What should people do?
  • Where must human judgment remain?
08

Growth & Meaning Layer

Connect change to purpose, values, goals, motivation, identity, and long-term growth.

Ask first
  • Why does this matter?
  • What future am I trying to create?
  • Does this align with my values?
09

CEAM+ Continuous Improvement Layer

Reflect, learn, restart after setbacks, and improve through repeated feedback cycles.

Ask first
  • What worked?
  • What caused the setback?
  • What smaller or better-supported step should come next?

Use the layers to understand your situation before choosing a tool or plan.

Research Agenda

The identity remains stable while the research questions continue evolving. Future technologies can be incorporated without changing the core mission.

Level 1 - Timeless Questions

How do people and organizations adopt, resist, and sustain change? What makes a choice feel possible? How do trust, learning, support, identity, and environment shape adaptation over time?

Level 2 - Decade Questions

How are current work, education, healthcare, rehabilitation, and community systems changing as AI becomes common? What practices help people adapt without increasing overwhelm?

Level 3 - Emerging Questions

What new questions are created by generative AI, automation, personalization, agentic tools, changing labor patterns, data systems, and new forms of human-machine decision-making?
