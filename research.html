Consulting Process

CEAM+ Research Architecture

CEAM+ is a framework and consulting platform for understanding how people, organizations, and markets adopt, resist, sustain, and adapt to change. AI is one important application area, but the broader CEAM+ research focus remains human adaptation, agency, trust, capability, resilience, sustainability, ethics, and flourishing.

CEAM+ provides a structured way to examine how contextual conditions, trusted signals, perceived meaning, capability, agency, and ethical judgment interact to shape varied responses to change over time. It generates testable propositions and practical questions rather than deterministic predictions.

Probabilistic framework

CEAM+ is an evolving framework and research program. It may generate probabilistic explanations and testable propositions, but uncertainty, heterogeneity, boundary conditions, and multiple pathways remain explicit.

"CEAM+ does not assume that individuals or organizations will respond to change in the same way, nor does it claim to predict every possible outcome. Human behavior, organizational conditions, environmental pressures, prior experiences, available resources, trust, agency, and other factors interact differently across contexts. CEAM+ therefore focuses on identifying patterns, conditions, pathways, and probabilities rather than deterministic outcomes. Unexpected, contradictory, and minority outcomes are treated as meaningful evidence that may reveal boundary conditions, missing variables, alternative explanations, or areas where the framework itself should be revised."

Scope, Status, and Connected Components

CEAM+ is not currently a universally validated scientific theory, a deterministic predictor of individual behavior, a clinical or psychological diagnostic tool, a universal explanation of all change, or proof that its components automatically produce better outcomes.

CEAM+ Research Framework

Contains constructs, theoretical relationships, propositions, measurements, boundary conditions, and validation plans.

CEAM+ Practice Method

Uses the framework to guide consulting conversations, change planning, implementation, reflection, and organizational learning.

CEAM+ Assessments

Contains developmental instruments informed by the framework. They are discussion aids, not validated diagnostic instruments unless and until validation evidence supports that designation.

These are connected components of one CEAM+ framework, not separate brands. Research claims and practice guidance should remain clearly labeled.

Five Core Construct Families

The empirical core groups related concepts into five construct families. Existing CEAM+ ideas remain available as subdimensions, moderators, mediators, contextual conditions, observable indicators, or outcomes rather than being treated as separate core constructs.

Perceived meaning and consequences

How people understand relevance, usefulness, risk, identity, emotion, purpose, and likely consequences.

Trusted signals

Which information, behavior, experiences, recommendations, events, or environmental cues are noticed, interpreted, and treated as credible, relevant, or legitimate.

Capability and opportunity

Skills, self-efficacy, cognitive capacity, time, resources, access, support, authority, and environmental conditions that make action possible or constrain it.

Agency and ethical acceptability

Meaningful choice, voice, perceived control, prudence, accountability, fairness, privacy, responsibility, and judgments about what should be done.

Adaptive response over time

Willingness, resistance, adoption, modification, delay, disengagement, learning, resilience, sustainability, and revision across time.

Supporting concepts such as trust, emotions, willingness, resistance, self-efficacy, resilience, prudence, accountability, invisible labor, sustainability, flourishing, environmental conditions, and early-warning indicators are classified according to the role they play in a specific study.

Epistemic Integrity

Epistemic integrity is a cross-cutting research and practice rule, not another CEAM+ layer.

Evidence and inference rule

“CEAM+ distinguishes observed behavior, self-reported experience, researcher interpretation, AI-generated interpretation, and causal inference. No single signal is treated as proof of an internal state, and credible alternative explanations must be considered before conclusions are drawn.”

Guard against distortion

  • Confirmation bias, sycophancy, and excessive agreement
  • Automation bias and treating AI interpretations as neutral
  • Correlation presented as causation
  • Emotional resonance presented as proof
  • False precision and generalization from individual cases

Keep behavior open to inquiry

  • Do not overread resistance, hesitation, silence, or compliance.
  • Do not assume adoption means acceptance or compliance means willingness.
  • Seek contradictory evidence and credible alternatives.
  • Specify in advance how heterogeneity could challenge, not merely protect, a proposition.
CEAM+ anti-distortion commitment

“CEAM+ does not treat agreement as evidence, confidence as accuracy, compliance as willingness, adoption as success, or a single signal as proof. The framework actively searches for contradictory evidence, alternative explanations, unequal power, measurement error, and unintended consequences.”

Trusted Signals

A signal is information, behavior, experience, recommendation, event, or environmental cue that a person or organization may use when interpreting change. Trusted signals are signals recipients perceive as sufficiently credible, relevant, legitimate, or consistent with experience to influence interpretation or response.

These are separate stages. Availability or exposure does not establish that a signal was noticed, understood, trusted, accepted, acted upon, or responsible for an outcome.

Possible methods

  • Likert-scale survey items and scenario-based questions
  • Interviews and focus groups
  • Behavioral observation
  • Communication records, organizational documents, and decision records
  • Longitudinal follow-ups and cross-source consistency checks
  • Multiple respondents from different organizational roles

Sample survey items

  • “I believe this source provides accurate information about the proposed change.”
  • “The information from this source is consistent with what I have personally observed.”
  • “I can question or challenge information from this source without negative consequences.”
  • “Different trusted sources are giving me consistent information about this change.”
  • “This source acknowledges uncertainty and limitations.”
  • “I would rely on this source when deciding how to respond to the change.”
Trust is not automatically beneficial

A trusted signal can be inaccurate, manipulative, incomplete, or harmful. High trust may improve coordination, while excessive or misplaced trust may reduce scrutiny.

Body, Emotional, and Behavioral Signals

Body language, microexpressions, resistance, hesitation, silence, and compliance are observable cues, not proof of an internal emotional state or intention. The same cue may have multiple explanations.

Silence could indicate

  • Reflection
  • Confusion
  • Fear or disagreement
  • Power imbalance
  • Cultural communication norms
  • Lack of information
  • Strategic compliance or disengagement

Triangulate before interpreting

  • Individual baseline and clusters of signals
  • Change over time and situational context
  • Cultural context and power relationships
  • Self-report and other evidence sources
  • Credible alternative explanations

Falsifiability

A framework capable of explaining every possible outcome after the fact has limited predictive value. CEAM+ propositions should state in advance what would support them, weaken them, or require revision.

CEAM+ Disconfirmation Principle

"CEAM+ propositions must identify evidence that would weaken, contradict, or require revision of the proposed relationship. Contradictory findings should not automatically be explained away as anomalies. They should be investigated as potential evidence of missing variables, boundary conditions, alternative explanations, measurement problems, or flaws in the framework itself."

Expected observation

What CEAM+ proposes should happen if the relationship is present.

Specified conditions

The population, context, timing, technology, culture, and implementation environment.

Support

Evidence that would increase confidence in the proposition.

Weakening evidence

Findings that make the proposition less likely, smaller, conditional, or incomplete.

Revision evidence

Findings that require CEAM+ to change the construct, pathway, boundary, or mechanism.

Competing explanations

Alternative variables that may better explain the observed pattern.

Probabilistic Relationships

CEAM+ propositions should generally use probabilistic and conditional language. Different individuals, organizations, industries, cultures, and environments may follow different pathways through change.

Use language such as

  • Increases the likelihood of
  • Is associated with
  • May strengthen
  • May weaken
  • Moderates
  • Mediates
  • Under specified conditions

Avoid universal claims such as

  • A always causes B.
  • Adoption always produces success.
  • Resistance is always irrational.
  • AI use always improves capability.
  • One pathway explains every organization.

Heterogeneity and Multiple Pathways

Variation between people is a central research interest, not a problem to eliminate. CEAM+ should investigate why people exposed to the same change respond differently and why apparently similar organizations produce different outcomes.

Immediate adoption

Why some people or organizations adopt quickly and continue.

Delayed adoption

Why some resist, watch, learn, and later adopt under changed conditions.

Abandonment

Why some initially adopt and later stop, either as failure or beneficial correction.

Successful resistance

Why some resistance protects people, quality, ethics, or resilience.

Dependency

Why some adoption increases reliance while reducing judgment or optionality.

Capability growth

Why some change strengthens agency, critical thinking, skill, and adaptation.

Minority pathway principle

"If 80 percent of participants follow one pathway and 20 percent follow another, CEAM+ should not automatically dismiss the 20 percent as noise. Minority outcomes may reveal mechanisms or conditions hidden by averages."

Boundary Conditions

CEAM+ should become more precise over time about where its propositions apply, where they weaken, and where they do not apply. Relationships may change across adoption settings and constraints.

Voluntary versus mandatory change Individual versus organizational adoption High versus low autonomy High versus low resource environments SMBs versus large organizations High risk versus low risk technologies AI versus non AI organizational change Different industries Different cultures Different regulatory environments Different levels of environmental uncertainty Different stages of adoption
Severe resource scarcity

Perceived usefulness may not result in adoption.

Coercive conditions

Observed adoption may not indicate willingness or acceptance.

Low leadership credibility

Positive leadership messages may increase resistance.

Reversible change

Perceived risk and resistance may decline.

Low authority or opportunity

Favorable attitudes may not influence behavior.

Contradictory trusted sources

Uncertainty may delay action.

Prior harm

Otherwise credible institutional signals may have less influence.

High trust in AI

Initial use may increase while scrutiny and independent verification decline.

Whenever possible, researchers should identify expected boundary conditions before analyzing outcomes and state what observations would weaken the proposed relationship.

Alternative Explanations

CEAM+ studies should consider plausible competing explanations before interpreting an observed association as support for a CEAM+ pathway. If higher agency is associated with higher sustained adoption, researchers should not immediately conclude that agency caused the outcome.

Management quality

Leadership behavior may explain adoption better than agency alone.

Resources and training

Teams with better support may show both higher agency and stronger outcomes.

Prior experience

Experienced users may adopt more easily independent of CEAM+ conditions.

Culture and job design

Norms, incentives, discretion, and workflow fit may shape sustained use.

Technology usability

A better tool may create apparent willingness without deeper trust or agency.

Self efficacy and selection

Confident or motivated participants may be more likely to choose the intervention.

Future CEAM+ research should compare competing explanations whenever possible through study design, measurement, statistical controls, qualitative comparison, or replication.

Proposed Initial Empirical Core

CEAM+ should not attempt to empirically test the entire framework at once. A smaller empirical pathway can serve as a starting model, not as a proven causal sequence. Individual components and relationships can be tested separately before attempting a larger integrated model.

Proposed Research Propositions

These preliminary propositions are examples for early CEAM+ studies. They are subject to revision and should be tested against alternative explanations, boundary conditions, and contradictory evidence.

These are propositions requiring empirical testing, not established findings.

P1 Greater perceived agency during implementation will be associated with a greater probability of sustained adoption when major contextual conditions are relatively comparable.
Expected relationship
Higher agency is associated with greater sustained adoption.
Possible moderators
Autonomy, mandate level, leadership support, workload, training quality.
Possible alternative explanations
Management quality, prior experience, self efficacy, job design, resources.
Evidence that would support it
Agency predicts sustained adoption after comparable conditions and relevant controls.
Evidence that would challenge it
Agency has no association, or the relationship disappears when training or management quality is included.
Evidence requiring CEAM+ revision
Higher agency consistently predicts worse outcomes or lower sustained adoption under conditions CEAM+ expected to be favorable.
P2 Greater cognitive overload during implementation will be associated with increased resistance and a lower probability of sustained adoption.
Expected relationship
Overload increases resistance and reduces sustained adoption.
Possible moderators
Task complexity, support availability, time pressure, prior skill, interface usability.
Possible alternative explanations
Poor tool fit, weak management, inadequate staffing, low perceived value.
Evidence that would support it
Overload measures predict resistance and lower retention across time.
Evidence that would challenge it
Overload is unrelated to resistance after usability and workload are measured.
Evidence requiring CEAM+ revision
High overload repeatedly predicts stronger sustained adoption without a clear learning or selection mechanism.
P3 High trust combined with low agency may increase the risk of dependency or automatic reliance under some conditions.
Expected relationship
Trust without agency may raise dependency risk.
Possible moderators
Automation level, review requirements, risk level, accountability, skill baseline.
Possible alternative explanations
Tool accuracy, organizational incentives, low training, time pressure, compliance culture.
Evidence that would support it
High trust and low agency predict lower verification, weaker explanation quality, or overreliance.
Evidence that would challenge it
High trust and low agency do not increase dependency when controls are included.
Evidence requiring CEAM+ revision
The combination reliably improves capability and judgment without dependency indicators across high-risk settings.
P4 Initial adoption will not necessarily predict sustained adoption when Conditions for Change are inadequate.
Expected relationship
Early use may decay when conditions are weak.
Possible moderators
Resource adequacy, workflow fit, leadership support, emotional readiness, habit compatibility.
Possible alternative explanations
Novelty effects, mandate effects, incentives, product usability, turnover.
Evidence that would support it
Initial adoption loses predictive strength when conditions are poor.
Evidence that would challenge it
Initial adoption strongly predicts sustained adoption regardless of measured conditions.
Evidence requiring CEAM+ revision
Conditions for Change consistently fail to explain any adoption durability across contexts.
P5 Higher technology adoption will not necessarily produce greater human or organizational benefit.
Expected relationship
Adoption and benefit are related but not equivalent.
Possible moderators
Ethics, capability effects, implementation fit, user autonomy, risk, work design.
Possible alternative explanations
Measurement mismatch, productivity tradeoffs, hidden workload, transition costs.
Evidence that would support it
High adoption appears with negative, mixed, or capability-reducing outcomes in some settings.
Evidence that would challenge it
Adoption consistently predicts benefit across visible and invisible metrics.
Evidence requiring CEAM+ revision
Multiple outcome categories add no explanatory value beyond adoption level.
P6 Resistance may sometimes represent an adaptive or protective response rather than implementation failure.
Expected relationship
Some resistance identifies risk, poor fit, ethical concern, overload, or harmful consequences.
Possible moderators
Risk level, psychological safety, expertise, history of failed change, regulatory context.
Possible alternative explanations
Status quo bias, fear, low skill, misinformation, political conflict.
Evidence that would support it
Resisters identify valid issues later confirmed by outcomes, incidents, or redesign needs.
Evidence that would challenge it
Resistance repeatedly fails to identify substantive risks and only delays beneficial change.
Evidence requiring CEAM+ revision
Protective resistance cannot be operationally distinguished from harmful resistance.
P7 Unexpected or minority outcomes may reveal boundary conditions or missing mechanisms that are not visible in aggregate results.
Expected relationship
Outliers and minority pathways reveal mechanisms hidden by averages.
Possible moderators
Sample diversity, implementation stage, local culture, resource variation, role differences.
Possible alternative explanations
Measurement error, random variation, small sample artifacts, data quality problems.
Evidence that would support it
Minority cases reveal repeatable conditions, mechanisms, or early warning indicators.
Evidence that would challenge it
Minority outcomes are non-repeatable and explained by error or noise after investigation.
Evidence requiring CEAM+ revision
CEAM+ cannot specify when minority outcomes should be investigated or how they inform propositions.
P8 Perceived credibility of a change signal will be more strongly related to willingness when the signal is consistent with direct experience.

Test consistency with experience as a moderator while comparing alternative explanations such as source familiarity, prior attitudes, and outcome quality.

P9 Opportunity to question a signal without punishment will strengthen the relationship between signal credibility and trust.

Test perceived and actual psychological safety, power differences, and whether challenge produces retaliation or meaningful response.

P10 Contradictory signals from highly trusted sources will increase uncertainty and delay adaptive response.

Measure source-specific trust, contradiction, uncertainty, timing, and whether clarification or coercion changes the pathway.

P11 Under resource scarcity, capability and opportunity will weaken the relationship between willingness and adoption.

Separate favorable attitudes from time, authority, access, skill, staffing, and other conditions required to act.

P12 Coerced use will be associated with observed adoption but not necessarily acceptance, trust, or sustained voluntary use.

Compare mandated behavior with self-report, later voluntary use, perceived choice, and power conditions.

P13 Reversibility will reduce perceived risk for some participants, with the effect depending on prior experiences and the consequences of failure.

Specify reversibility, prior harm, stakes, and who bears the cost of failed experimentation before testing.

P14 Passive delegation to AI will be more strongly associated with capability erosion than active collaboration.

Compare explanation quality, independent task performance, verification, skill retention, and authorship over time.

P15 High trust in AI will improve willingness to use it up to a point, after which additional trust may reduce verification and increase authority transfer.

Test a possible nonlinear relationship; frequent use alone is not evidence of dependence or authority transfer.

P16 Preserved agency will be associated with greater psychological ownership and sustainability, subject to organizational and task conditions.

Measure meaningful choice and actual authority separately, and identify organizational and task boundary conditions in advance.

AI Reliance and Authority Transfer

Frequent AI use does not automatically equal dependence. CEAM+ examines the quality and consequences of reliance, including who understands, verifies, challenges, and retains final authority.

Functional reliance

AI assists with a task while the person retains understanding and authority.

Cognitive dependence

A person increasingly struggles to perform or evaluate the task without AI.

Epistemic authority transfer

A person treats AI as the final judge of what is accurate or true.

Moral authority transfer

A person allows AI to determine what ought to be done.

Relational displacement

AI begins replacing necessary human connection, disagreement, accountability, or support.

Capability erosion

Skills, confidence, situational awareness, or independent judgment weaken through repeated passive delegation.

Productive collaboration

AI expands capability while the person retains authorship, comprehension, verification, agency, and final responsibility.

Possible indicators

  • Ability to explain the final output
  • Ability to complete or evaluate the task without AI
  • Verification behavior
  • Willingness to challenge AI recommendations
  • Consultation with qualified humans
  • Distress when AI is unavailable
  • Retention of relevant skills
  • Confidence in independent judgment
  • Understanding of how the conclusion was reached
  • Who retains final decision authority
  • Whether AI expands or narrows meaningful human participation
AI-use commitment

“AI may assist with analysis, pattern identification, and communication, but it does not receive automatic factual, interpretive, moral, or decision-making authority. Human reviewers remain responsible for verifying evidence, examining alternatives, and making consequential decisions.”

Multiple Outcome Principle

CEAM+ must never assume: more adoption = more success. The framework evaluates the quality and consequences of change, not merely whether adoption occurred.

Operational effectivenessAdoption or useSustainabilityEthical acceptabilityAgency preservationHuman capabilityPsychological ownershipWork meaningfulnessIndividual and organizational resilienceHuman flourishing

These outcomes may conflict. A change can improve efficiency while reducing agency, meaningfulness, privacy, trust, or resilience. They should not be combined into one success score unless the weighting method is transparent, justified, and tested.

High adoption + positive outcomes

Change is used and appears beneficial.

High adoption + negative outcomes

Use spreads while harm, overload, inequity, or dependency rises.

Low adoption + negative outcomes

Change fails to take hold and produces cost or disruption.

Low adoption + positive outcomes

Selective non-adoption protects quality, ethics, capability, or fit.

Adoption + declining human capability

Use increases while skill, judgment, or critical thinking weakens.

Adoption + increasing human capability

Use strengthens learning, agency, and adaptive capacity.

Resistance + protective outcome

Pushback prevents harm or improves design.

Resistance + harmful outcome

Pushback blocks a valuable change without identifying valid risk.

Abandonment + beneficial correction

Stopping prevents waste or harm after evidence changes.

Abandonment + implementation failure

Stopping reflects poor support, fit, trust, or conditions.

Flourishing principle

“CEAM+ examines whether change strengthens or weakens people’s ability to live, learn, choose, connect, contribute, and adapt. Growth, adoption, and efficiency are not treated as automatically beneficial.”

Learning and Revision Loop

CEAM+ treats revision as a feature of good research rather than evidence that the framework has failed. Weak signals, rare outcomes, near misses, deviations from averages, and early warning indicators should feed the learning loop.

Research Integrity Rules

These rules protect CEAM+ from becoming an after-the-fact explanation machine. They also preserve the framework's emphasis on ethics, resilience, human capability, and visible and invisible metrics.

  • Do not treat correlation as causation.
  • Do not claim universality from one population.
  • Do not discard minority outcomes simply because they are uncommon.
  • Do not redefine constructs after seeing the results merely to protect a hypothesis.
  • Do not treat adoption itself as proof of success.
  • Do not assume resistance is irrational.
  • Do not assume technological efficiency equals human benefit.
  • Do not hide contradictory findings.
  • Clearly distinguish observed evidence from interpretation.
  • Clearly distinguish established theory from new CEAM+ propositions.
  • Clearly distinguish exploratory findings from confirmatory findings.
