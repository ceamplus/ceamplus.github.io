Consulting Process

CEAM+ Research Architecture

CEAM+ is a framework and consulting platform for understanding how people, organizations, and markets adopt, resist, sustain, and adapt to change. AI is one important application area, but the broader CEAM+ research focus remains human adaptation, agency, trust, capability, resilience, sustainability, ethics, and flourishing.

This research architecture makes CEAM+ more measurable, falsifiable, and empirically testable without claiming that complex human or organizational behavior can be perfectly predicted.

Probabilistic framework

CEAM+ is a probabilistic, adaptive framework rather than a deterministic prediction model.

"CEAM+ does not assume that individuals or organizations will respond to change in the same way, nor does it claim to predict every possible outcome. Human behavior, organizational conditions, environmental pressures, prior experiences, available resources, trust, agency, and other factors interact differently across contexts. CEAM+ therefore focuses on identifying patterns, conditions, pathways, and probabilities rather than deterministic outcomes. Unexpected, contradictory, and minority outcomes are treated as meaningful evidence that may reveal boundary conditions, missing variables, alternative explanations, or areas where the framework itself should be revised."

Evidence

CEAM+ should be evaluated through multiple forms of evidence rather than relying on one survey, one case, or one research method. Future studies should deliberately search for evidence that supports the framework and evidence that challenges it.

Established foundations

Existing literature, established theory, validated constructs, and citation-mapped concepts. [Citation placeholder]

Quantitative evidence

Surveys, experiments, quasi experiments, outcome measures, behavioral and usage data, and longitudinal models.

Qualitative evidence

Interviews, field observations, case studies, implementation narratives, negative cases, and organizational histories.

Signals and deviations

Weak signals, outliers, near misses, minority outcomes, early warning indicators, and deviations from averages.

Contradictory evidence

Unexpected consequences, disconfirming results, alternative explanations, measurement problems, and failed propositions.

Research use

Evidence should refine CEAM+ concepts, test propositions, locate boundary conditions, and improve practical consulting judgment.

  • Existing literature and established theory
  • Quantitative surveys
  • Qualitative interviews
  • Behavioral and usage data
  • Experiments and quasi experiments
  • Organizational and individual case studies
  • Longitudinal research
  • Outcome measures
  • Negative cases
  • Weak signals
  • Outliers and minority outcomes
  • Unexpected consequences
  • Contradictory evidence
Evidence principle

"Evidence that contradicts CEAM+ is not automatically noise. Contradictions may reveal missing variables, alternative pathways, boundary conditions, measurement problems, or weaknesses in the framework itself."

Falsifiability

A framework capable of explaining every possible outcome after the fact has limited predictive value. CEAM+ propositions should state in advance what would support them, weaken them, or require revision.

CEAM+ Disconfirmation Principle

"CEAM+ propositions must identify evidence that would weaken, contradict, or require revision of the proposed relationship. Contradictory findings should not automatically be explained away as anomalies. They should be investigated as potential evidence of missing variables, boundary conditions, alternative explanations, measurement problems, or flaws in the framework itself."

Expected observation

What CEAM+ proposes should happen if the relationship is present.

Specified conditions

The population, context, timing, technology, culture, and implementation environment.

Support

Evidence that would increase confidence in the proposition.

Weakening evidence

Findings that make the proposition less likely, smaller, conditional, or incomplete.

Revision evidence

Findings that require CEAM+ to change the construct, pathway, boundary, or mechanism.

Competing explanations

Alternative variables that may better explain the observed pattern.

Probabilistic Relationships

CEAM+ propositions should generally use probabilistic and conditional language. Different individuals, organizations, industries, cultures, and environments may follow different pathways through change.

Use language such as

  • Increases the likelihood of
  • Is associated with
  • May strengthen
  • May weaken
  • Moderates
  • Mediates
  • Under specified conditions

Avoid universal claims such as

  • A always causes B.
  • Adoption always produces success.
  • Resistance is always irrational.
  • AI use always improves capability.
  • One pathway explains every organization.

Heterogeneity and Multiple Pathways

Variation between people is a central research interest, not a problem to eliminate. CEAM+ should investigate why people exposed to the same change respond differently and why apparently similar organizations produce different outcomes.

Immediate adoption

Why some people or organizations adopt quickly and continue.

Delayed adoption

Why some resist, watch, learn, and later adopt under changed conditions.

Abandonment

Why some initially adopt and later stop, either as failure or beneficial correction.

Successful resistance

Why some resistance protects people, quality, ethics, or resilience.

Dependency

Why some adoption increases reliance while reducing judgment or optionality.

Capability growth

Why some change strengthens agency, critical thinking, skill, and adaptation.

Minority pathway principle

"If 80 percent of participants follow one pathway and 20 percent follow another, CEAM+ should not automatically dismiss the 20 percent as noise. Minority outcomes may reveal mechanisms or conditions hidden by averages."

Boundary Conditions

CEAM+ should become more precise over time about where its propositions apply, where they weaken, and where they do not apply. Relationships may change across adoption settings and constraints.

Voluntary versus mandatory change Individual versus organizational adoption High versus low autonomy High versus low resource environments SMBs versus large organizations High risk versus low risk technologies AI versus non AI organizational change Different industries Different cultures Different regulatory environments Different levels of environmental uncertainty Different stages of adoption

Alternative Explanations

CEAM+ studies should consider plausible competing explanations before interpreting an observed association as support for a CEAM+ pathway. If higher agency is associated with higher sustained adoption, researchers should not immediately conclude that agency caused the outcome.

Management quality

Leadership behavior may explain adoption better than agency alone.

Resources and training

Teams with better support may show both higher agency and stronger outcomes.

Prior experience

Experienced users may adopt more easily independent of CEAM+ conditions.

Culture and job design

Norms, incentives, discretion, and workflow fit may shape sustained use.

Technology usability

A better tool may create apparent willingness without deeper trust or agency.

Self efficacy and selection

Confident or motivated participants may be more likely to choose the intervention.

Future CEAM+ research should compare competing explanations whenever possible through study design, measurement, statistical controls, qualitative comparison, or replication.

Proposed Initial Empirical Core

CEAM+ should not attempt to empirically test the entire framework at once. A smaller empirical pathway can serve as a starting model, not as a proven causal sequence. Individual components and relationships can be tested separately before attempting a larger integrated model.

  2. Trust / Trusted Signals
  3. Agency / Perceived Control
  4. Willingness or Resistance

Proposed Research Propositions

These preliminary propositions are examples for early CEAM+ studies. They are subject to revision and should be tested against alternative explanations, boundary conditions, and contradictory evidence.

P1 Greater perceived agency during implementation will be associated with a greater probability of sustained adoption when major contextual conditions are relatively comparable.
Expected relationship
Higher agency is associated with greater sustained adoption.
Possible moderators
Autonomy, mandate level, leadership support, workload, training quality.
Possible alternative explanations
Management quality, prior experience, self efficacy, job design, resources.
Evidence that would support it
Agency predicts sustained adoption after comparable conditions and relevant controls.
Evidence that would challenge it
Agency has no association, or the relationship disappears when training or management quality is included.
Evidence requiring CEAM+ revision
Higher agency consistently predicts worse outcomes or lower sustained adoption under conditions CEAM+ expected to be favorable.
P2 Greater cognitive overload during implementation will be associated with increased resistance and a lower probability of sustained adoption.
Expected relationship
Overload increases resistance and reduces sustained adoption.
Possible moderators
Task complexity, support availability, time pressure, prior skill, interface usability.
Possible alternative explanations
Poor tool fit, weak management, inadequate staffing, low perceived value.
Evidence that would support it
Overload measures predict resistance and lower retention across time.
Evidence that would challenge it
Overload is unrelated to resistance after usability and workload are measured.
Evidence requiring CEAM+ revision
High overload repeatedly predicts stronger sustained adoption without a clear learning or selection mechanism.
P3 High trust combined with low agency may increase the risk of dependency or automatic reliance under some conditions.
Expected relationship
Trust without agency may raise dependency risk.
Possible moderators
Automation level, review requirements, risk level, accountability, skill baseline.
Possible alternative explanations
Tool accuracy, organizational incentives, low training, time pressure, compliance culture.
Evidence that would support it
High trust and low agency predict lower verification, weaker explanation quality, or overreliance.
Evidence that would challenge it
High trust and low agency do not increase dependency when controls are included.
Evidence requiring CEAM+ revision
The combination reliably improves capability and judgment without dependency indicators across high-risk settings.
P4 Initial adoption will not necessarily predict sustained adoption when Conditions for Change are inadequate.
Expected relationship
Early use may decay when conditions are weak.
Possible moderators
Resource adequacy, workflow fit, leadership support, emotional readiness, habit compatibility.
Possible alternative explanations
Novelty effects, mandate effects, incentives, product usability, turnover.
Evidence that would support it
Initial adoption loses predictive strength when conditions are poor.
Evidence that would challenge it
Initial adoption strongly predicts sustained adoption regardless of measured conditions.
Evidence requiring CEAM+ revision
Conditions for Change consistently fail to explain any adoption durability across contexts.
P5 Higher technology adoption will not necessarily produce greater human or organizational benefit.
Expected relationship
Adoption and benefit are related but not equivalent.
Possible moderators
Ethics, capability effects, implementation fit, user autonomy, risk, work design.
Possible alternative explanations
Measurement mismatch, productivity tradeoffs, hidden workload, transition costs.
Evidence that would support it
High adoption appears with negative, mixed, or capability-reducing outcomes in some settings.
Evidence that would challenge it
Adoption consistently predicts benefit across visible and invisible metrics.
Evidence requiring CEAM+ revision
Multiple outcome categories add no explanatory value beyond adoption level.
P6 Resistance may sometimes represent an adaptive or protective response rather than implementation failure.
Expected relationship
Some resistance identifies risk, poor fit, ethical concern, overload, or harmful consequences.
Possible moderators
Risk level, psychological safety, expertise, history of failed change, regulatory context.
Possible alternative explanations
Status quo bias, fear, low skill, misinformation, political conflict.
Evidence that would support it
Resisters identify valid issues later confirmed by outcomes, incidents, or redesign needs.
Evidence that would challenge it
Resistance repeatedly fails to identify substantive risks and only delays beneficial change.
Evidence requiring CEAM+ revision
Protective resistance cannot be operationally distinguished from harmful resistance.
P7 Unexpected or minority outcomes may reveal boundary conditions or missing mechanisms that are not visible in aggregate results.
Expected relationship
Outliers and minority pathways reveal mechanisms hidden by averages.
Possible moderators
Sample diversity, implementation stage, local culture, resource variation, role differences.
Possible alternative explanations
Measurement error, random variation, small sample artifacts, data quality problems.
Evidence that would support it
Minority cases reveal repeatable conditions, mechanisms, or early warning indicators.
Evidence that would challenge it
Minority outcomes are non-repeatable and explained by error or noise after investigation.
Evidence requiring CEAM+ revision
CEAM+ cannot specify when minority outcomes should be investigated or how they inform propositions.

Multiple Outcome Principle

CEAM+ must never assume: more adoption = more success. The framework evaluates the quality and consequences of change, not merely whether adoption occurred.

High adoption + positive outcomes

Change is used and appears beneficial.

High adoption + negative outcomes

Use spreads while harm, overload, inequity, or dependency rises.

Low adoption + negative outcomes

Change fails to take hold and produces cost or disruption.

Low adoption + positive outcomes

Selective non-adoption protects quality, ethics, capability, or fit.

Adoption + declining human capability

Use increases while skill, judgment, or critical thinking weakens.

Adoption + increasing human capability

Use strengthens learning, agency, and adaptive capacity.

Resistance + protective outcome

Pushback prevents harm or improves design.

Resistance + harmful outcome

Pushback blocks a valuable change without identifying valid risk.

Abandonment + beneficial correction

Stopping prevents waste or harm after evidence changes.

Abandonment + implementation failure

Stopping reflects poor support, fit, trust, or conditions.

Learning and Revision Loop

CEAM+ treats revision as a feature of good research rather than evidence that the framework has failed. Weak signals, rare outcomes, near misses, deviations from averages, and early warning indicators should feed the learning loop.

Research Integrity Rules

These rules protect CEAM+ from becoming an after-the-fact explanation machine. They also preserve the framework's emphasis on ethics, resilience, human capability, and visible and invisible metrics.

  • Do not treat correlation as causation.
  • Do not claim universality from one population.
  • Do not discard minority outcomes simply because they are uncommon.
  • Do not redefine constructs after seeing the results merely to protect a hypothesis.
  • Do not treat adoption itself as proof of success.
  • Do not assume resistance is irrational.
  • Do not assume technological efficiency equals human benefit.
  • Do not hide contradictory findings.
  • Clearly distinguish observed evidence from interpretation.
  • Clearly distinguish established theory from new CEAM+ propositions.
  • Clearly distinguish exploratory findings from confirmatory findings.
