# CEAM+ Measurement Dictionary

Updated 2026-09-21. This register describes authored screening items and exploratory diagnostics. Published source frameworks support conceptual choices; their validation does not transfer to CEAM+ items. No CEAM+ scale is claimed empirically validated.

## Measurement architecture

Construct → operational definition → indicator → question/metric → response scale → evidence type → scoring → interpretation → recommended action.

The Marketing Assessment preserves the 40 supplied screening statements and adds fourteen separate construct evidence reviews. These distinguish organizational belief, reported/observed practice, measured data, outcomes and source/method. No numerical composite is fabricated across these different units. The numerical eight-dimension screening profile remains explicitly self-reported and provisional.

## Marketing screening items

All 40 use the supplied ordinal 1–5 response scale. Arithmetic means and maturity bands are transparent descriptive conventions, not interval-scale proof or validated thresholds. Each question is an organization informant’s perception/report of capability or practice. None is a direct customer observation or measured performance outcome. Unknown and unanswered responses are excluded with coverage disclosed.

## Marketing construct evidence reviews

### Market Orientation

- Definition: Generation, internal dissemination and organizational response to market intelligence.
- Why it matters: Distinguishing market orientation from adjacent capabilities prevents a general marketing impression from substituting for specific evidence.
- Indicators: intelligence generation; intelligence dissemination; responsiveness.
- Question: What customer or competitor intelligence was gathered, who received it, and what decision changed as a result?
- Corroborating metric: Dated intelligence reports linked to recipients and decisions.
- Types: perceptual, behavioral, operational, outcome-based.
- Scale: Record belief, observed practice, measured data and outcome separately. Quantities retain their original units, denominator and reporting period; qualitative evidence retains source context.
- Scoring: No composite score is assigned to this evidence dossier. The separate 40-item screening scale remains 1–5 self-report only.
- Interpretation: A reported belief is not evidence of practice. Documented practice is not proof of an outcome. Missing evidence remains unknown. Sources are supplied by the user and not independently verified.
- Weak/missing evidence: Gather the named evidence before inferring capability.
- Contradictory evidence: Compare conflicting sources, segments and periods.
- Next action: Trace one market finding through a cross-functional decision and its outcome.
- Status: CEAM+ exploratory operationalization informed by the cited framework; not the published validated scale.
- Limits: Selection, recall, single-informant bias, missing denominators and confounding can limit interpretation. Reliability and validity have not been established for these items.
- Conceptual source: [Kohli, Jaworski & Kumar (1993), MARKOR](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/002224379303000406).

- Interpretation: A reported belief is not evidence of practice. Documented practice is not proof of an outcome. Missing evidence remains unknown. Sources are supplied by the user and not independently verified.

## AI Use & Adoption Analysis

This is mode 2 of ai-adoption, version 2.0-analysis. Existing readiness version 1.0 and its saved results are preserved. The new mode has eight independent practice profiles, each with three original exploratory items. No overall AI score is calculated. The 1–5 mean describes reported practice; observed outcomes are separate evidence records. Every finding distinguishes Observed Evidence, Supported Inference, Proposed Application, and Unknown. Observed means reported and traceable to the user-supplied source, not independently verified. A claimed observation without a source is treated as Unknown.

Evidence confidence is categorical. Unsupported/proposed/contradictory evidence is Low. Documented sourced evidence can be Moderate. High requires an observed, objective behavioral/financial/long-term outcome, strong declared measurement quality and at least two distinct declared source references. These are heuristic rules, not estimated probabilities, proof of source independence, or validated confidence estimates.

## Qualitative AI records and decision rules

- Customer journey: six stages; AI/human contribution, mechanism, expected outcome, customer/organization benefit, friction, trust, personalization, human interaction, unintended consequences and source classification remain separate.
- Human Necessity: eight explicit questions per activity; allocation is reviewer-supplied or conservatively suggested from explicit whole-task judgments. Conflicting AI-led and cannot/should-not responses produce a review flag. Merely mentioning a human capability does not prove its necessity.
- Marketing mix: optional 4Ps and service 7Ps with evidence classification on every entry. Proposals never become claims of current use.
- Governance: risk, stakeholder, consequence, current/recommended safeguards, accountable party, responsibility level and sources. An empty register does not prove safety.
- Perception–Evidence Gap: activity/engagement metrics cannot substantiate financial or long-term claims. The software reports the observed metric, not the benefit claim as proven.
- Stress tests: assumption, failure impact, revision trigger, fallback and owner. These are scenarios, not forecasts or probabilities of black-swan events.
- Baseline comparison: independent human text and case are locked before analysis. Comparison is word/topic overlap only. Candidates for shared or unmatched findings require human review; the tool cannot reliably determine semantic agreement or who missed evidence.
- Case extraction: exact source lines with keyword-based topic/metric candidates, no automatic factual verification, no externally fetched case data. Case text is not interpreted as instructions.

## Research program before validation claims

1. Expert review of construct coverage, item relevance, dimensional distinctions and domain boundaries.
2. Cognitive interviews across businesses, nonprofits, sectors, sizes and respondent roles.
3. Pilot data with informed consent, source provenance and repeat administrations.
4. Assess missingness, response bias, test–retest stability and inter-rater agreement.
5. Pre-specify reflective versus formative measurement; do not treat Cronbach’s alpha alone as validity or apply it indiscriminately across different constructs.
6. Test factor structure, convergent/discriminant validity, measurement invariance where appropriate, and external criterion/predictive validity on separate data.
7. Recalibrate thresholds and decision rules against observed outcomes; version every material change and retain prior scoring semantics.
