CEAM+ Adoption Model

CEAM+ Human Adaptation Assessments

The CEAM+ Adoption Model is an applied component of the broader CEAM+ framework. It is designed to examine conditions that may influence how individuals and organizations approach, adopt, resist, sustain, abandon, or adapt to change.

The model considers interacting factors such as trust, trusted signals, agency, perceived control, willingness, resistance, environmental conditions, resources, cognitive demands, and readiness for change.

CEAM+ does not assume that every person or organization will follow the same pathway or reach the same outcome. Assessment results should therefore be interpreted as patterns, conditions, and areas for further inquiry rather than deterministic predictions.

Understand how information, emotions, agency, trust, environment, implementation, human-AI roles, purpose, and improvement affect your next decision.

Start with Personal / Daily Life or Work / Business. Existing education, care, and adaptive-support assessments remain available as broader applications of the framework.

Developmental, distortion-aware assessment

These instruments are informed by the CEAM+ Research Framework but are not validated diagnostic instruments. Scores are discussion indicators, not diagnoses or proof of character, motivation, resistance, ethics, capability, or willingness. No self-report instrument can eliminate lying, misunderstanding, social desirability, or impression management.

Interpret with uncertainty

Results include limitations and alternative explanations. Contradictory answers create follow-up questions rather than accusations.

Triangulate when stakes are high

Use self-report alongside context, behavior over time, organizational records, and perspectives from different roles. No single response is proof of an internal state.

Keep structural barriers visible

Interpret time, resources, access, authority, culture, power, and opportunity before placing responsibility on an individual.

Preserve human authority

Any AI-generated interpretation is provisional. A human reviewer must verify evidence, examine alternatives, and retain final decision authority.
